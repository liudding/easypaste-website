Enable macOS Built-in Clipboard History (Tahoe): Free Taster

Updated 2026-07-24 · ~5 min read · For macOS Tahoe users

Starting with macOS Tahoe (26), Apple quietly added a clipboard history inside Spotlight. No app to install — you can recover what you just copied. This walkthrough shows you how to turn it on, plus its retention window and limits.

1. How to enable it

  1. Anywhere on screen, hold ⌘ (Command) and press Space to open Spotlight;
  2. Keeping ⌘ held, press 4;
  3. The first time, macOS asks to enable it — click "Enable";
  4. After that, ⌘Space → ⌘4 shows your recent copies.

Press Enter from Spotlight to paste an item back — and pasting this way strips formatting, a handy trick for clean plain-text paste.

2. Retention: 8 hours, extendable to 7 days

Default retention is about 8 hours. In System Settings → Spotlight, macOS 26.1 lets you adjust it: as short as 30 minutes (more private) up to 7 days. Anything older is auto-cleared.

3. Its limitations

  • Text only: images, files, and colors are not recorded;
  • No cross-device sync: copies on iPhone/iPad don't show on Mac;
  • Weak search: browse by time, not millisecond keyword search like dedicated tools;
  • Limited entries: heavy users will outgrow it fast.

4. When to upgrade to a dedicated tool

If you only occasionally recover a just-copied line, the built-in history is enough. But upgrade when you need to store images/files/colors, cross-device iCloud sync, instant search across thousands of items, or a password ignore list. FAQ

1. My Mac doesn't have this?▾

Clipboard history requires macOS Tahoe (26) or later. Earlier versions have no such feature — you'll need a third-party tool or a system upgrade.

2. Does the built-in history log passwords?▾

Apple automatically skips sensitive sources like Apple Passwords, so passwords generally aren't recorded. If you still want finer control, a dedicated tool's ignore list can exclude specific apps.
