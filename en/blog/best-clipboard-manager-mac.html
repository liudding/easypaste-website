Why You Need a Clipboard Manager

The default Mac clipboard is a single slot. Copy a link, then copy an address, and the link is gone. For casual users that is a minor annoyance. For anyone who copies more than a few things a day, it is a small tax paid dozens of times.

Maya, a product designer in Lisbon, learned this the hard way last spring. She copied a six-color palette from a client moodboard into Figma, then grabbed a hex code from a Slack message. When she went back for the palette, it had evaporated. She rebuilt it from memory — close, but not exact, and the client noticed. A clipboard manager that records images and colors would have saved her an awkward call.

The 2026 twist: macOS Tahoe has one built in

Most 2024 roundups missed this: macOS 26 Tahoe quietly added a built-in clipboard history inside Spotlight. Press ⌘Space (keep Command held), then tap ⌘4. The first time, macOS asks if you want to turn it on. Say yes, and from that moment Spotlight keeps a log of what you copy.

A few facts worth knowing:

  • It is free and already on your Mac — no download required.
  • Retention is 8 hours by default, and in macOS 26.1 Apple added settings to extend it to 7 days (or shrink it to 30 minutes for more privacy) under System Settings → Spotlight.
  • Pasting from Spotlight strips formatting, which is a handy trick for pasting clean text.
  • It does not sync to iPhone or iPad, and it stores text only — no images, no files.

So is the built-in tool enough? For light users, yes. But it cannot search thousands of items, it ignores images and files, it expires, and it stays trapped on one Mac. That is the gap every app below fills. How We Picked

We did not rank these by vibes. Every tool below was scored on seven criteria that actually matter day to day:

  1. History depth — how many items it stores and for how long.
  2. Search speed — can you find the thing you copied three days ago in two keystrokes?
  3. Multi-device sync — does your clipboard follow you to iPhone, iPad, or a second Mac?
  4. Format support — text, images, files, links, code, colors.
  5. Price — free, one-time, or subscription, and what you get for it.
  6. Privacy — local-only vs cloud-by-default, and whether it can exclude sensitive apps.
  7. Native feel — does it behave like a Mac app, or fight the operating system?

We favored tools that stay quiet in the menu bar and respect your privacy. The good ones earn their place without nagging.

In-Depth Reviews

1. Paste — The Beautiful One

Win: The most gorgeous clipboard manager on macOS, with a visual timeline and Pinboards that feel like magic.

Gripe: It is a subscription, defaults to the cloud, and uses more memory than lighter rivals.

For: Designers and presenters who care about interface and want iPhone/iPad sync via iCloud.

Paste (pasteapp.io) is the app Apple might have built if Apple designed for delight first. It shows your history as a flowing ribbon of cards, previews links as webpages, and even pulls text out of images with OCR. Search is fast, pinning is effortless, and sync across Mac, iPhone, and iPad works through iCloud. The catch is price: $29.99/year (or a $90 lifetime, or via Setapp). It also stores your history in the cloud by default, which privacy-minded users should weigh. If you want the deep head-to-head, our Maccy vs Paste deep dive breaks it down.

2. Maccy — The Free Speed Demon

Win: Blazing fast, keyboard-first, and completely free and open source.

Gripe: No cloud sync, and deliberately minimal — what you see is what you get.

For: Anyone who wants a free clipboard manager mac that stays invisible until called.

Maccy (maccy.app) is the quiet hero of this list. Hit a shortcut, start typing, press Enter — your item is pasted. Fuzzy search means you rarely type more than two characters. It is native, lightweight, and respects your privacy because everything stays local. The honest limitation: there is no sync, so your history lives on one Mac. If you can live without cross-device sync, Maccy is the best free clipboard manager mac you can install today.

3. Raycast — The All-in-One Launcher

Win: A launcher, a clipboard history, snippets, window management, and thousands of extensions in one app.

Gripe: Clipboard is an add-on, and you have to adopt the whole launcher mindset.

For: Power users who want one app to rule search, commands, and clipboard.

Raycast (raycast.com) is less a clipboard manager than a command center that happens to include one. Its Raycast clipboard manager captures text, images, colors, and links, and the free tier keeps a generous history (roughly three months). Go Pro at $8/month for unlimited history, AI, and cloud sync. The trade-off: if you are happy with Spotlight or Alfred, adopting Raycast just for clipboard feels heavy. But if you already live in it, the clipboard is a free bonus.

4. Alfred — The Scriptable Veteran

Win: Mature, deeply scriptable, and a one-time Powerpack unlock.

Gripe: Clipboard lives behind the paid Powerpack, and there is a learning curve.

For: Automators who build custom workflows and want them to last.

Alfred is the granddaddy of Mac launchers. The core app is free, but clipboard history, snippets, and file actions require the Powerpack (£34 one-time, or £59 Mega for lifetime updates). Once unlocked, you get searchable history, dynamic snippets, and workflow automation no other tool matches. The downside is commitment: Alfred's power hides behind configuration. If you just want clipboard history without learning a system, Maccy or EasyPaste is kinder.

5. CopyClip / CopyClip 2 — The Plain-Text Minimalist

Win: Zero-cost, zero-config, sits quietly in the menu bar.

Gripe: Text only — no images, no files, no fancy features.

For: People who copy words and nothing else.

CopyClip is the dictionary definition of "good enough." The original is free; CopyClip 2 costs about $7.99 and adds app exclusions so sensitive apps (banking, password managers) never log. It will not win awards, but it will never get in your way. If your clipboard life is pure text, this is the lightest possible upgrade over macOS's built-in tool.

6. Clipy — The Customizable Open-Source Option

Win: Free, open source, and packed with snippets and blacklist controls.

Gripe: The interface feels dated and some builds lack strong search.

For: Tinkerers who want snippets plus history without paying.

Clipy is a community revival of the old ClipMenu. It stores text and images, manages snippets, and lets you blacklist apps. It is free and capable, but the UI has not aged gracefully, and search is weaker than Maccy's. Still, for a free clipboard manager mac with snippet support, it earns a spot.

7. Flycut — The Developer's Friend

Win: Free, open source, and built around text, URLs, and code.

Gripe: No images or tables; history caps around ~99 items.

For: Engineers who copy code snippets all day.

Diego, a backend engineer we spoke with, once lost a 12-line SQL migration he had copied from a Stack Overflow answer. He copied a terminal command right after, and the snippet was gone. He rewrote it — and shipped a bug that took an afternoon to trace. Keyboard-first tools like Flycut and Maccy exist so a copied command never erases the snippet you needed. Flycut keeps plain text, URLs, and code, supports multi-paste, and can back up history to Dropbox. No images, but for code, that is fine.

8. Unclutter — The Three-in-One

Win: Clipboard plus notes and file shelf in one gesture-friendly panel.

Gripe: Clipboard capability is limited; it is not a dedicated manager.

For: Neat freaks who want one tray for everything.

Unclutter is three tools in one: a notes pad, a file staging shelf, and a basic clipboard history, all revealed by a trackpad gesture at the top of the screen. It syncs notes and files via Dropbox, but the clipboard part is thin compared to dedicated apps. Think of it as a tidy catch-all, not your primary clipboard engine.

9. Yoink — A Drag-and-Drop Shelf (Not a Clipboard Manager)

Win: Makes drag-and-drop workflows feel effortless across Mac and iPad.

Gripe: It is not a clipboard history tool — do not expect searchable copy history.

For: Anyone who stages files and images between apps.

Yoink deserves a mention as a related efficiency tool, but we are flagging it clearly: Yoink is not a clipboard manager. It is a drag-and-drop shelf where you park files and images while moving them between windows or devices. If your real need is "I copied something and it disappeared," Yoink will not help. FAQ

1. How do I enable the built-in Mac clipboard history?▾

On macOS 26 Tahoe or later, open Spotlight with ⌘Space, then press ⌘4 (keep Command held and tap Space then 4). The first time, macOS asks to enable it. You can adjust retention (8 hours to 7 days) in System Settings → Spotlight. For a visual walkthrough, see enable macOS built-in clipboard history.

2. Are clipboard managers safe? Do they log passwords?▾

Reputable managers store history locally and let you exclude sensitive apps (banking, password managers). macOS Tahoe's built-in history already skips passwords copied from Apple Passwords. Avoid unknown tools, and prefer local-first apps such as Maccy or EasyPaste. Never paste a one-time password into a tool you do not trust.

3. Is a free clipboard manager enough?▾

For most people, yes. Maccy and EasyPaste prove you do not need to pay for fast search, history, and even iCloud sync. Pay only if you want Paste's polish, OCR, or team features. A free clipboard manager mac covers 90% of real workflows.

4. Maccy vs Paste — which is better?▾

Maccy wins on price (free), speed, and privacy (local-only). Paste wins on design, iCloud sync, images, and OCR. If you do not need sync, Maccy is the smarter pick. If you want the prettiest synced experience and do not mind a subscription, choose Paste. Full breakdown in our Maccy vs Paste deep dive.

5. Which clipboard managers support iPhone/iPad sync?▾

Paste syncs across Mac, iPhone, and iPad via iCloud. EasyPaste (the free macOS clipboard manager) syncs to your Apple devices through iCloud as well — for free. The built-in Tahoe history, Maccy, Raycast, and Alfred do not sync clipboard to mobile devices.

6. Do clipboard managers slow down my Mac?▾

Lightweight ones do not. Maccy, Flycut, and EasyPaste use minimal memory and idle until you call them. Heavier apps like Paste use more resources because of cloud sync and rich previews, but on any modern Mac the impact is small. If your Mac feels sluggish, check the app's history limit and excluded apps.

7. Is Raycast's or Alfred's clipboard enough, or do I need a separate one?▾

If you already use Raycast or Alfred daily, their built-in clipboard is probably enough — Raycast's is free and strong; Alfred's needs the Powerpack. A separate app makes sense if you do not use a launcher, want lighter footprint, or need mobile sync those launchers lack.
